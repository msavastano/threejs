# HOLD ON — a 20-second motion reel that is also a rollercoaster

`rollercoaster-reel.mp4` — 1920×1080, 60 fps, 20.000 s, stereo techno. Every pixel and every
sound is generated by code in this folder: no Hyperframes, no motion-graphics library, no
samples, no stock footage. (Chromium is only used as a canvas to draw on and ffmpeg only
to encode the frames.)

The reel *is* a ride. Its structure, its music and its on-screen HUD (a tiny coaster whose
track profile is the edit) all follow the same track:

| time        | bars | the ride            | picture                                             | music (144 BPM) |
|-------------|------|---------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|-----------------|
| 0.00–3.13   | 0–1  | **lift hill**       | one-take side view; cart lurches on every chain clack, `HOLD ON!` slams in letter by letter, riders' hearts pound | chain-lift clacks, endless-rising Shepard tone, heartbeat, snare roll |
| 3.13–3.33   |      | **the hang**        | camera pushes onto three terrified faces; ECG flat-lines | near silence (a reverse swell) |
| 3.33–5.00   | 2    | **the drop**        | camera whips into the plunge, speed lines, `WHOA!`  | impact, kick, rolling bass |
| 5.00–6.67   | 3    | first-person 3D     | procedural neon coaster: drop → vertical loop        | arp joins |
| 6.67–8.33   | 4    | kinetic type        | FAST · LOUD · LOOPY · WILD!, one beat each           | hats, toms fill |
| 8.33–10.00  | 5    | **airtime**         | weightless riders, metaballs, calliope notes as bubbles | kick & bass drop out; calliope + pad |
| 10.00–11.67 | 6    | the build           | 3·2·1, collapsing rings, then a second hang          | rising arp, riser, snare roll |
| 11.67–14.17 | 7–8  | **second drop**     | shape-morph grid · 12-fold kaleidoscope · voxel cube | acid line, supersaw stabs |
| 14.17–15.00 |      | corkscrew           | first-person, 720° roll                              | |
| 15.00–15.83 | 9    | control room        | gauge · equalizer (driven by the real mix) · scope · squash-and-stretch ball | key lift |
| 15.83–16.67 |      | close-ups           | four faces cut on eighth notes                        | |
| 16.67–18.33 | 10   | everything at once  | whole ride from above, three comets racing it         | fill into the hit |
| 18.33–20.00 | 11   | **the station**     | cart brakes in, station bell, confetti, `AGAIN?`     | final hit, brake hiss, bell |

144 BPM × 12 bars is exactly 20.000 s, so every beat lands on a computable time.

## What's in here

```
rollercoaster-reel.mp4        the video (H.264 + AAC)
soundtrack.wav                the music on its own (48 kHz, 16-bit)
src/audio/make_audio.py       synthesises the soundtrack (numpy + numba) and writes reel/score.js
src/reel/                     the motion-graphics engine (open index.html for a live preview)
    lib.js        math, easing, noise, palette, and score helpers (pulse / since / band)
    font.js       a display typeface drawn from scratch: skeleton strokes with round caps
    chars.js      the three riders: one parametric face/body rig
    g3d.js        3D engine + turtle-graphics coaster (loop, corkscrew, banked turns)
    post.js       WebGL2 finishing pass: bloom pyramid, chromatic aberration, glitch, grain
    timeline.js   the edit: shot list, transitions, HUD, per-frame post parameters
    scene_*.js    the shots
    score.js      generated: every hit of the soundtrack + measured band envelopes
src/render.py                 renders 1200 frames in parallel, then encodes with ffmpeg
```

## Why picture and sound are locked

`make_audio.py` writes `score.js`: the exact time of every kick, clap, clack, bass note, stab,
bell… and 240 Hz energy envelopes measured from the final mix. The engine reads it, so the
picture doesn't merely *follow* the beat — it is driven by the actual events: letters land on
the chain clacks, the cart lurches on them, the countdown numerals are the four kicks, the
equalizer panel is the real spectrum of the track, the confetti fires on the station bell.

Everything is a pure function of time `t` (no accumulated state), which is what lets frames be
rendered in any order, in parallel, with true motion blur: each output frame averages 4–6
sub-frames inside a 180° shutter.

## Rebuild

```bash
pip install numpy scipy numba pyloudnorm playwright imageio-ffmpeg
python3 src/audio/make_audio.py     # -> soundtrack.wav + src/reel/score.js   (~10 s)
python3 src/render.py               # -> rollercoaster-reel.mp4               (~10 min on 4 cores)
```

Or open `src/reel/index.html` in a browser and press play for a real-time preview (no motion
blur; it plays `soundtrack.wav` in sync).
